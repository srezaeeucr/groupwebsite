📡 Super-Resolving Galaxy Spectra with Physics-Informed AI ● Ongoing

Developing a physics-informed artificial intelligence framework to reconstruct higher-resolution galaxy spectra from low-resolution observations. Spectral resolution fundamentally determines the level of physical information extractable from galaxy spectra—high-resolution data allow clean separation of emission lines and enable robust measurements of redshift, star-formation rates, chemical abundances, and ionization conditions.

Methodology

  • Training on paired JWST/NIRSpec data from the JADES survey
  • Learning from galaxies observed in both prism (low-res) and medium-resolution grating modes
  • Model learns statistical and physical relationships between paired spectra
  • Recovers narrow emission-line structure blended in original low-resolution data

Figure: Comparison of a low-resolution prism spectrum (purple) with the AI super-resolved spectrum (orange). The model recovers emission-line structure that is blended at low spectral resolution, demonstrating substantial improvements in emission-line fidelity and signal-to-noise.

Results

  • Reconstructed spectra show substantial improvements in emission-line fidelity and signal-to-noise
  • Model closely reproduces structure present in higher-resolution observations
  • Successfully evaluated on galaxies not used during training
  • Scalable pathway for extracting high-resolution information from large low-resolution surveys

Future Applications

  • Direct relevance for Euclid mission spectroscopic data
  • Application to Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope surveys
  • Maximizing scientific return from large-area, low-resolution surveys

Manifold Learning for Galaxy Populations ● Published

Advanced application of Self-Organizing Maps (SOMs) to analyze high-dimensional galaxy spectral energy distributions and scaling relations. This approach enables us to understand the underlying structure of galaxy populations and predict spectroscopic properties from photometric observations.

Methodology

  • Trained SOMs on photometric data from CANDELS survey fields
  • Mapped KECK MOSDEF spectroscopic data onto the manifold
  • Analyzed redshift bins at z ∼ 1.5 and z ∼ 2.2
  • Examined optical+near-infrared SED space patterns

Results

  • AGNs map to more massive, less dusty regions (confirming selection biases)
  • Successfully predicted spectroscopic features from photometry
  • Confirmed mass-metallicity relations through manifold analysis
  • SOMs outperformed linear interpolation in photometric predictions
